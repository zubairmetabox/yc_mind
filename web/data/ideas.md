### 1. Agentic claims-adjusting firm for SMB property & casualty insurance
**What it does:** An AI-native claims-adjusting firm (not a SaaS tool sold to adjusters — an actual adjusting firm) that inspects damage claims, negotiates settlements with carriers, and files disputes on behalf of SMB property owners and public-adjuster shops, using agentic workflows for the negotiation loop itself, not just paperwork.
**Why now:** `firm` is rising sharply (+1.3pts, 0.1%→1.4%) but every current example (PARES AI, Denki, Automax.ai, Cranston AI) replaces accounting/appraisal/real-estate firms — none touch claims adjusting, where the negotiation-with-carrier step is exactly the kind of multi-turn, stakes-bearing reasoning agentic systems are now credible at.
**Target customer:** Public adjuster shops and SMB commercial property owners who can't afford in-house claims counsel.
**Risk:** Carriers may refuse to negotiate with an AI counterparty or flag accounts that use it, killing the core loop.

### 2. Autonomous inspection drones for midstream oil & gas pipeline compliance
**What it does:** Drone fleets that autonomously fly fixed pipeline/tank-farm routes, detect corrosion/leaks via multispectral imaging, and auto-generate the regulatory compliance filings (PHMSA-style) operators are required to submit — output is the filing, not just the imagery.
**Why now:** `drones` (+1.3pts) and `infrastructure` (+1.6pts) are both rising, but current drone examples are defense (Perseus Defense, Seeing Systems) or novelty/agri (Tornyol, GrazeMate) — none target the unglamorous, heavily-regulated industrial inspection niche where the real budget (avoiding fines) sits.
**Target customer:** Midstream oil & gas operators' compliance and asset-integrity teams.
**Risk:** Regulatory bodies may not accept AI-generated filings without a licensed engineer sign-off, capping the automation value at "draft, not file."

### 3. Decision-audit layer for agentic underwriting and lending systems
**What it does:** A compliance-and-audit layer that sits between an insurer's or lender's agentic underwriting models and the regulator, capturing the reasoning trail behind every automated approve/deny/price decision in a format that satisfies fair-lending and insurance-rate-filing requirements.
**Why now:** `layer` (+1.9pts) and `agentic` (+2.0pts) are both rising fast, but every `layer` example (GhostEye, Lark, TraceRoot.AI, Scott AI) targets engineering reliability/security — none target the regulatory-liability gap that opens the moment agentic systems start making real underwriting decisions.
**Target customer:** Compliance teams at mid-size insurers and non-bank lenders deploying agentic underwriting.
**Risk:** Regulators may move slower than the underlying agentic-underwriting adoption, leaving no urgent compliance deadline to sell against yet.

### 4. Autonomous prior-authorization and appeals agent for specialty pharmacies
**What it does:** An agent that submits prior-authorization requests and automatically files appeals on denials for specialty pharmacies, using each payer's specific denial-reason codes and historical win patterns to draft the appeal language.
**Why now:** This explicitly leans on a **falling theme as an underserved-niche signal**: `automating` is cooling overall (-0.2pts) but the remaining examples (Perspectives Health, Amera, Norra, Scoop) show admin-automation concentrating into narrow, high-friction verticals rather than disappearing — specialty pharmacy prior-auth (among the highest-denial-rate categories in US healthcare) isn't yet claimed.
**Target customer:** Billing/reimbursement teams at specialty pharmacies (oncology, rare disease).
**Risk:** Payers could route around denial-pattern automation by simply rotating denial reasons faster than the model adapts.

### 5. AI-native land survey and title firm using autonomous drones
**What it does:** A licensed survey-and-title firm (not software sold to surveyors) that runs autonomous drone-based boundary and topographic surveys, then auto-generates the legal survey documents and title-ready plats for real estate developers.
**Why now:** Combines two rising themes — `firm` (+1.3pts) and `drones` (+1.3pts) — and is distinct from the existing `firm` examples, all of which are appraisal/accounting (Automax.ai, PARES AI), not the upstream survey/title step that gates every land deal.
**Target customer:** Mid-size real estate developers doing land entitlement, and county recorders' offices needing faster plat turnaround.
**Risk:** Survey licensing is state-regulated and slow to recognize drone-based methods as legally sufficient — could face multi-year approval lag per state.

### 6. Agentic dispatch and code-compliance documentation for licensed trades
**What it does:** An agentic operating layer for HVAC/electrical contractor franchises that handles dispatch and scheduling *and* auto-generates the code-compliance documentation/permit paperwork required after each job — the permitting step, which licensed trades currently do manually or skip.
**Why now:** `agents` (+5.7pts) is the single biggest riser, but the closest existing example (NOSO LABS) is field-technician diagnosis/sales, not the back-office permitting/compliance burden that franchise owners describe as their actual pain.
**Target customer:** Multi-location HVAC/electrical/plumbing franchise operators (10+ trucks).
**Risk:** Permitting requirements vary by municipality with no standard API, making the long tail of jurisdiction coverage a slow, services-heavy build.

### 7. Autonomous IP prior-art and freedom-to-operate research firm
**What it does:** An AI-native firm (replacing the patent-research paralegal/IP-boutique function, not selling them software) that runs long-horizon autonomous prior-art search and freedom-to-operate analysis for biotech and hardware startups ahead of a funding round or product launch.
**Why now:** `autonomous` (+1.7pts) examples so far (Arcten, Thesis) are generic "autonomous AI research" labs with no vertical wedge; wrapping the same long-horizon-research capability around the specific, billable IP-research workflow turns a research capability into a firm with a clear price point.
**Target customer:** Biotech/hardware seed-to-Series-A startups preparing for fundraising or launch who currently pay $15–40k to IP boutiques.
**Risk:** Freedom-to-operate opinions carry legal liability — an "AI firm" without attorney sign-off may not be defensible as a real substitute, capping it to a pre-screening tool rather than the firm itself.

### 8. Agentic escrow and closing-coordination firm for real estate transactions
**What it does:** An AI-native firm that coordinates multi-party real estate closings — chasing documents from lenders, title companies, and agents, resolving scheduling conflicts, and flagging missing conditions — as an agentic replacement for a closing coordinator, not appraisal or valuation.
**Why now:** Combines `real` (+2.1pts) and `firm` (+1.3pts); existing `firm` examples in real estate (PARES AI, Automax.ai) are valuation/appraisal-focused, leaving the higher-friction, more-coordination-heavy closing process — the step buyers/agents complain about most — untouched.
**Target customer:** Independent real estate agents and small brokerages who currently rely on overworked human closing coordinators.
**Risk:** Title companies and lenders may resist exposing the document/status APIs an agentic coordinator needs, since they currently own that coordination relationship.

## Notes on methodology
- All 8 ideas were checked against the example companies listed in `data/idea_prompt.md` for their theme(s) to avoid re-pitching an existing YC company.
- 6 of 8 ideas explicitly combine two rising themes per the prompt's preference for intersections over single-trend bets.
- Idea #4 explicitly draws on a **falling** theme (the prompt's permitted exception) — used because the few remaining examples revealed a concentrating niche, not a disappearing one.
- Recurring meta-pattern across these ideas: the highest-signal gap right now is **"AI-native professional-services firm" wrapping agentic/autonomous capability around a specific regulated, billable function** (claims adjusting, surveying, IP research, closing coordination) — these are firms, not SaaS sold to the incumbent firms.
